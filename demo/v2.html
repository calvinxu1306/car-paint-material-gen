Car paint from several photos

A network looks at photos of one paint swatch, taken by a camera that stays put while the light moves: the phone's flash, then lights from the side. It estimates every layer: the base coat, metallic flakes, the clear coat and its tint (candy paint), and the thin film that makes pearl and colour-shift paint change colour with angle. None of these samples was used for training.

How the sphere approximates Blender: the thin film uses three.js's iridescence (film IOR, one thickness), and a predicted film thinner than 50 nm is drawn as no film. three.js works out the film's colour from the viewing angle alone (Blender from the angle between light and viewer) and as if the film faced air rather than lay under the clear coat, so the colours are close to Blender's, not exact. three.js has no clear-coat tint, so a candy coat is drawn by multiplying the base colour by tint² (light crosses the coat twice); in Blender the tint also deepens towards grazing angles. Orange peel is not drawn.

Light

One light, placed relative to your view: elevation 5° and azimuth 0° put it next to the camera, like the phone's flash. Swing it round to see pearl and colour-shift paints change colour. "Light only" is the dark room of the training photos, where the clear coat has nothing but the light to reflect.
